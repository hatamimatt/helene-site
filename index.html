Research

My research focuses on hydroclimate extremes and risk: how floods, droughts, extreme precipitation and heatwaves arise, and how infrastructure and ecosystems can stay resilient to them under deep uncertainty.

Extremes

Characterizing and modeling hydroclimatic extremes

Floods, droughts, extreme precipitation and heatwaves: the processes behind them and how to model them, with a focus on the southeastern United States.

Decisions

Decisions under deep uncertainty

Stochastic decision frameworks that improve the resilience of infrastructure and ecosystems when the future climate cannot be pinned down.

Vulnerability

Climate vulnerability across sectors

Process-based and ensemble approaches to assess climate vulnerability across water, energy, transportation, agriculture and public health.

Field notes.

Notes and interactive pieces on extreme rainfall, hydroclimate risk, and the data and tools behind them.

About

I am a PhD student at the University of Florida, in the Climate Resilience Lab of Dr. Nasser Najibi’s research group in the Department of Agricultural and Biological Engineering.

I began my PhD in Civil Engineering at the University of South Carolina in the iWERS lab with Dr. Erfan Goharian, alongside graduate coursework in computer science, and moved to UF when my advisor left. Before that, I spent four months at the University of Groningen building an agent-based model of human behavior during cascading disasters.

I earned an MSc in Civil Engineering for Risk Mitigation at Politecnico di Milano, with a thesis on deep learning and remote sensing in disaster management, and a BSc in Civil Engineering at Imam Khomeini International University, where river hydrology and hydraulics started my interest in flood risk.
